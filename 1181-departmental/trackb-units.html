Units — Track B: Digital Rhetoric & Social Media

The class broken into units. Each unit is a major assignment plus the readings and activities that feed it. This is the same material as the schedule, cut a different way — by assignment rather than by week.

Readings named by role — "the data reading," "the reflection reading" — are chosen by the instructor. See the Readings page for what each slot needs to do and what currently fills it.

Unit 1 — Project 1: The Conversation Essay

Students put their own digital habits in conversation with the readings. Roughly Weeks 2–6.

Readings

  • The data reading — research or survey data on how people actually use digital media — CORE
  • The argument reading — a short essay taking a position on digital life and the self. Offer at least two options; students annotate one. — CORE
  • Reading: 3.5 Academic Writing as a Genre — CORE
  • Reading: 10.4 Weaving Personal Experience into Academic Writing — CORE
  • At least one from Chapter 2, reading strategies — CORE
  • At least one from Chapter 4 (4.1–4.3), the writing process — CORE
  • Critical GenAI literacy: at least 1–2 readings, including at least one from Chapter 7 — CORE

The GenAI reading lands in this unit because Project 1 is the first assignment carrying a GenAI use policy.

Activities

Full instructions on the Writing Activities page.

  • WA1: Reading Annotation — Digital Habits
  • WA2: Your Online Habits
  • WA3: Process Freewrite

Sequence

  1. Rough draft, minimum 700 words
  2. Peer reading — YEPs / NOPEs / WOWs
  3. Final draft, minimum 1,000 words
  4. Optional revision, with a written account of what changed

Unit 3 — Project 3: The Argument Essay

The cumulative project. Students argue a position on the course question using readings from across the semester. Roughly Weeks 11–14.

Readings

  • The topic strand readings — offer two or more strands so students choose a topic. Each strand needs one required reading students annotate and one reference reading they skim. — CORE
  • Reading: 3.6 Understanding Essays — CORE
  • Reading: 4.5 Revising & Orders of Concern — CORE
  • Reading: 8.4 Revision Strategy: Reverse Outline — FLEX
  • Reading: 8.6 Editing and Proofreading; 12.2 MLA Formatting — FLEX

Students also reuse the argument reading they annotated in Unit 1 — Project 3 requires it.

Activities

  • WA6: Reading Annotation — YEPS / NOPES / WOWS
  • WA7: Argument Outline
  • WA8: Self-Assessment

Sequence

  1. Outline (WA7)
  2. Rough draft, minimum 1,100 words
  3. Peer reading — YEPs / NOPEs / WOWs
  4. Self-assessment (WA8)
  5. Final draft, minimum 1,200 words
  6. Optional revision

What Depends on What

Read this before swapping anything. These interlocks aren't obvious from any single assignment.

  • The reading a student annotates in WA1 is the reading they use in Project 1 — and again in Project 3. Changing the argument reading options changes all three.
  • WA5 (Scrolling Log) is where Project 2's text comes from. Students choose their analysis subject out of what they logged. Cut WA5 and students arrive at Project 2 with nothing to analyze.
  • WA7 (Argument Outline) is Project 3's prewriting. The assignment sheet tells students to use it.
  • WA3 (Process Freewrite) and WA8 (Self-Assessment) feed the Final Reflection. They're deliberately low-stakes; their value is what students can look back at at the end of the semester.
  • The annotation in WA6 uses the same YEPS / NOPES / WOWS frame as peer review. Students learn one feedback vocabulary and apply it to published writing and to each other. Changing one and not the other loses that.
