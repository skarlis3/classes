Writing Activities (Flex)

Note to instructorsThese Writing Activities scaffold toward Project 1 and Project 2. You may modify, replace, or add writing activities as needed — these are starting points.

WA1: Rhetorical Situation Observation

  • Read 3.2 Rhetorical Situation.
  • Identify the rhetorical situation (purpose, audience, speaker, context) of 2–3 different texts. Your texts can be anything: a meme, a news article, an assignment description, a menu, etc.
  • When referencing ideas from the reading, use signal phrases (e.g., "According to the reading..." or "The author explains that...") to distinguish between the reading's ideas and your own.
  • ~200–300 words or structured response

WA2: Genre Observation

  • Read 3.3 Navigating Genres.
  • Choose a genre you encounter in daily life (text messages, emails, Instagram posts, job postings, etc.) and describe its conventions. What makes it recognizable as that genre?
  • Reminder: use signal phrases when referencing ideas from the reading.
  • ~200–300 words

WA3: GenAI & Course Policies

  • Read the GenAI reading (TBD) and review the course's GenAI policy.
  • Write about your experience with generative AI so far: Have you used it? For what? What do you think it does well? What concerns do you have? How does the course policy shape how you'll use (or not use) it this semester?
  • ~200–300 words

WA4: Field / Career Selection

  • Choose a field or career you're interested in (or currently working in) to focus on for the semester. Write a short essay explaining what the field is, why you're interested in it, and what you already know about the kind of work people do in it.
  • ~200–300 words
  • Not sure where to start? Some examples: nursing, automotive technology, criminal justice, early childhood education, business, IT, dental hygiene, welding, culinary arts, etc.

WA5: Field Exploration

  • Research what types of writing and communication happen in your chosen field. Find at least 3 different genres. Describe each one briefly: what it is, who writes it, who reads it, and what it's for.
  • It may take several different search terms to find the genres in your field. Some search terms to try: "types of writing in [your field]," "[job title] job description" (look for writing tasks listed under responsibilities), and "what do [job title]s write"
  • If your field's genres are especially hard to find or are mostly standardized forms: Look at how professionals in your field present themselves to clients — professional websites, service descriptions, client-facing materials
  • ~200–300 words

WA6: Genre Selection & Initial Analysis

  • Choose one genre from your field exploration. Find one real sample of that genre. Describe its purpose, audience, and context.
  • Upload the sample as a PDF or screenshot.
  • ~200–300 words

WA7: Sample Collection & Observations

  • Find at least 3 samples of your chosen genre. Look at them side by side and identify patterns in content, organization, tone, style, and design.
  • Upload all samples.
  • Write a list or short paragraphs describing what you notice.

WA8: Visual Rhetoric Observation

  • Read 6.1 What are Multimodal Texts? and 6.2 Understanding Visual Rhetoric.
  • Find an example of a multimodal text (a website, poster, infographic, social media post, etc.) and analyze how the visual design choices affect how the audience reads and understands the text.
  • ~200–300 words

WA9: Academic Writing

  • Read 3.5 Academic Writing as a Genre and 3.6 Understanding Essays.
  • Respond to the following. Be specific and use examples from the readings and from your own experiences:
    • Which concepts from the readings seemed most important to you?
    • Which concepts surprised you or made you think differently about college writing?
  • ~200+ words

WA10: Peer Review Preparation

  • Read 4.4 In-Class Peer Review.
  • Write 200+ words reflecting on peer review: What are your experiences with peer review? What are your concerns about getting or giving feedback? Based on the reading, what can you do to give good feedback?

Reflection & Checklist

  • Reflect on the writing and revision process so far. Self-evaluate your revised draft against the assignment requirements before submitting.

WA: GenAI & Genre

Note to instructorsThis WA should be assigned after students have collected their genre samples (WA7 or later), so they have real examples to compare against the AI-generated output.

  • Use a generative AI tool to produce a complete sample of your genre. (If your genre is typically longer than a page or two, you can focus on one section, but make sure it's substantial enough to analyze.) Submit a transcript or screenshot of your full conversation with the AI tool along with your response.
  • In your response, analyze the AI-generated sample:
    • What did the AI seem to assume about the rhetorical situation — the audience, purpose, and context? Do those assumptions match how this genre actually works in your field?
    • Where did the AI fabricate or "fill in" information it wasn't given — specific details, data, sources, claims, names? How would you know this if you weren't already familiar with the genre?
    • What would happen if someone used this output as-is in a real professional setting? What's at stake — for the writer, the audience, or the field?
  • ~300–400 words (not counting the transcript)
