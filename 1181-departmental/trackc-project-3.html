Project 3: PSA Poster

Note to instructors

The capstone. It asks students to make an argument in a purely visual form, using a logical claim drawn from a reading they’ve already worked with. The “no new research” rule is what forces them back into the earlier units. Both the poster and its reflection are required for credit.

Overview

You’ll create a single-image PSA poster that persuades a student audience about how — or how not — to use generative AI for schoolwork. It brings together everything from the semester: rhetorical situation, the three appeals, visual rhetoric, and responsible GenAI use.

Both parts of this assignment — the poster and the reflection — must be submitted to receive credit.

Task

Create a single-image poster, flyer, or one-panel infographic that:

  • Targets a specific student audience you choose, and says which — middle school, high school, undergraduate, graduate.
  • Takes an explicit persuasive stance: instructing that audience how to or how not to use GenAI for educational purposes.
  • Incorporates ethos, pathos, and logos. Your logos must come from one of the articles already assigned in Project 1 or Project 2, and you must name that article in your reflection. No outside research or outside sources.
  • Is your original work. You may use a design tool, draw by hand and scan or photograph it, or use simple layout software — but you may not submit text or images produced by generative AI as the finished PSA.
  • Is submitted as an image file posted where your classmates can see it, not as a link or an attachment that requires extra clicks.

Design and Content Requirements

  • The poster must include a clear headline or slogan, at least one image, and a logical claim tied to the assigned article.
  • Do not paste GenAI-written text into your final poster. You may use a GenAI tool to brainstorm phrasing or layout ideas only after your prompt has been reviewed, and you must cite anything you used in your reflection.
  • If you hand-draw, make sure the image is clear and readable.

Criteria for Success

  • Rhetorical clarity and audience targeting — the audience is explicit and your choices match it; the purpose is clear
  • Ethos, pathos, logos — all three are present and effective, and logos is clearly tied to an assigned article
  • Visual rhetoric and design — layout, hierarchy, readability, and visual choices help persuade rather than distract
  • Integrity and process — the poster is original, you followed the GenAI rules, and you submitted it in the required form

Reflection

Paired with the poster (WA11). Covers audience and rhetorical situation, how each appeal works in your design, two specific design choices and why they persuade this audience, transparency about any GenAI use, and a closing evaluation of the poster’s effectiveness plus one next step.

What Feeds This

  • The visual rhetoric and multimodality readings, revisited from Project 1
  • The prompt-drafting workshop (WA11’s discussion counterpart), where students test and refine a GenAI brainstorming prompt with peer feedback
  • Any article from Project 1 or Project 2 — the source of your logical claim
