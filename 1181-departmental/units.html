Units — Track A: Genres in Your Field

The class broken into units. Each unit is a major assignment plus the readings and activities that feed it. This is the same material as the schedules, cut a different way — by assignment rather than by week.

Unit 1 — Project 1: Genre Analysis Essay

The long unit. Students pick a field, find the genres in it, narrow to one, gather samples, identify conventions, and write the analysis. Roughly Weeks 2–11.

Readings

  • Reading: 3.2 What is the Rhetorical Situation? — CORE
  • Reading: 3.3 Navigating Genres — CORE
  • Reading: 3.5 Academic Writing as a Genre — CORE
  • Reading: 3.6 Understanding Essays — CORE
  • Reading: 4.5 Revising & Orders of Concern — CORE
  • At least one from Chapter 2, reading strategies — CORE
  • At least one from Chapter 4 (4.1–4.3), the writing process — CORE
  • Critical GenAI literacy: at least 1–2 readings, including at least one from Chapter 7 — CORE
  • Reading: 6.1 What are Multimodal Texts? — CORE
  • Reading: 6.2 Understanding Visual Rhetoric — CORE

Why visual rhetoric comes this early

Students choose their own genre from their own field, so there's no predicting what they'll land on. Some will end up with a genre that's substantially about design — a website, a brochure, an infographic, a client-facing one-pager, a social media account — and they need this vocabulary to analyze its conventions in the essay itself, not after it's written.

Students whose genre turns out to be text-only don't lose anything: these are the readings behind Project 2, so everyone uses them again in Unit 2.

  2. Peer review (or feedback from an ASC tutor)
  3. Revised draft
  4. Final draft

In the no-portfolio model the revised draft is the graded version, with an optional final revision allowed for a better grade. In the portfolio model the final draft is bundled with the Final Reflection at the end of the semester.

What Depends on What

Read this before swapping anything. These interlocks aren't obvious from any single assignment.

  • WA4 through WA7 build Project 1 in sequence. Field selection feeds field exploration, which feeds genre selection, which feeds sample collection. Skipping one leaves the next without input.
  • The GenAI & Genre activity needs WA7 first. Students compare AI-generated output against genre samples they've actually gathered; without the samples there's nothing to compare against.
  • Visual rhetoric comes before Project 1 drafting, even though it's Project 2's material. Students pick their own genres, and some of those genres are largely design. Moving these readings later strands the students who needed them to write the essay.
  • Project 2 builds on Project 1's genre work. Students apply concepts they developed across the whole first unit, so Project 2 can't move earlier without moving everything.
  • Reflection & Checklist feeds the Final Reflection. It's where students first evaluate their own work against the requirements.
