Project 1: Genre Analysis Essay

Overview

You'll write a 1,300–1,700 word (~5–7 page) essay analyzing one genre from a professional field. Your essay will examine the genre's conventions, purpose, audience, and how it functions within its professional context.

Choosing Your Genre

Pick a genre from your career field or a field you're interested in — for example, things people in that field write or produce as part of their work (e.g., police reports, patient chart notes, client proposals, lesson plans).

If your field's genres are especially hard to find or are mostly standardized forms: Look at how professionals in your field present themselves to clients — professional websites, service descriptions, client-facing materials.

If you're undecided on your major, choose a field that you find interesting or are considering.

A good genre for this assignment is one where the writer makes real choices about how to communicate. If it's a standardized form where you just fill in blanks, keep looking.

Prompt

Write an analysis essay that analyzes a specific genre in your field.

In your analysis, include an introduction your field and the genre, explain the typical audience and purpose for this genre, analyze a minimum of three conventions of the genre, and discuss how the genre functions in this field.

What Your Essay Needs to Include (Prompt Breakdown)

  • A brief introduction to the professional field
  • A description of the genre
  • An analysis of the genre's purpose and audience.
    • Note: Your genre may have more than one audience!
  • An analysis of at least 3 conventions — specific, observable patterns you found across your samples (not broad categories like "tone" or "structure")
  • An analysis of how the genre functions in professional practice
  • Evidence from at least 3 real samples of the genre. Each convention should be supported by more than one example from one of these samples.

Format

  • MLA style
  • 1,300–1,700 words (not including Works Cited)
  • In-text citations identifying the source of each sample
  • Works Cited page

Drafting Sequence

You'll build toward the final essay through multiple drafts:

  1. Full Rough Draft (1,300–1,700 words)
  2. Peer Review (or feedback from an ASC tutor)
  3. Revised Draft (revised since peer review)
  4. Final Draft
