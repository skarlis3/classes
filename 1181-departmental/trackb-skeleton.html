DRAFT

About This Track

This track focuses on one question, which the whole semester answers: How are our digital lives affecting our real lives?

Three projects plus a final reflection:

  • Project 1: The Conversation Essay — a personal essay putting the student's own experience in conversation with the readings.
  • Project 2: The Digital Rhetoric Essay — a rhetorical analysis of one piece of media pulled from the student's own feed.
  • Project 3: The Argument Essay — an argument drawing on the course readings.
  • Final Reflection.

Nine writing activities scaffold the projects, alongside Introductions and a structured peer reading. Every project runs the same cycle — read, activity, rough draft, peer reading, final draft, optional revision.

Adapted with permission from a colleague's course. The original runs as an 8-week online section; this is the transferable version — no fixed dates, no LMS-specific instructions, and no modality assumed.

See Units for how the readings and activities group around each project.

Required Assignments & Readings (Core)

Core assignments and readings ensure that the course outcomes and objectives are met and also help provide continuity for students taking the first-year writing sequence at Macomb. These cannot be changed without departmental approval (see ENGL 1181 Assignment Change Request Form).

Required Readings

  • Reading: 2.4 Responding to Texts
  • Reading: 3.1 What is Rhetoric?
  • Reading: 3.2 What is the Rhetorical Situation?
  • Reading: 3.5 Academic Writing as a Genre
  • Reading: 3.6 Understanding Essays
  • Reading: 4.6 Revising & Orders of Concern
  • Reading: 6.1 What are Multimodal Texts?
  • Reading: 6.2 Understanding Visual Rhetoric
  • Reading: 6.3 Visual Rhetoric and Digital Writing
  • Reading: 10.4 Weaving Personal Experience into Academic Writing

Also, include at least one from each of the following categories:

  • Reading strategies: At least one from Chapter 2 (2.1–2.3)
  • Writing process: At least one from Chapter 4 (4.1–4.3)
  • Critical GenAI literacy: At least 1–2 readings, including at least one from Chapter 7

This track also requires four readings chosen by the instructor rather than named here — a data reading, an argument reading, digital identity readings, and a reflection reading — plus a set of topic-strand readings. See Readings for what each slot needs to do and what currently fills it.

Required Major Assignments

  • Project 1: The Conversation Essay — the student's own experience in conversation with assigned readings
  • Project 2: The Digital Rhetoric Essay — rhetorical analysis of one piece of digital media the student chooses
  • Project 3: The Argument Essay — an argument answering the course question, supported by course readings
  • Peer Review (a structured peer reading of a draft before each project's final version)
  • Final Reflection (end of semester)

Required Minor Assignments or In-Class Activities

If you use the Writing Activities as written, all outcomes and objectives will be covered. If you adjust or replace any of them, the following still need covering in your replacements or as in-class work:

  • Close reading and annotation (currently WA1 and WA6)
  • Prewriting and outlining (currently WA5 and WA7)
  • Self-assessment and process reflection (currently WA3 and WA8, which feed the Final Reflection)

Recommended Assignments & Readings (Flex)

Flex assignments and readings support the core assignments and help ensure all course objectives are covered. These can be altered, replaced, or supplemented without departmental approval, as long as the core projects and objectives remain supported.

Since some of these cover the outcomes/objectives in the class as the primary assignment/activity, ensure that those are still covered if any of these assignments are altered. (See "Required Minor Assignments or In-Class Activities" section above for more information.)

Writing Activities

Writing Activities (WAs) scaffold toward the three projects. Instructors may modify, replace, or add as needed. See the Writing Activities page for the full list.

Optional but recommended

There is no required number of Writing Activities — use all of them, some of them, or none at all. Unlike some flex assignments, these can be dropped entirely, not just replaced.

They're recommended because they break the major projects into steps, and each one produces something students reuse, so no project starts from a blank page. If you drop them, plan on scaffolding that work another way — the objectives listed under Required Minor Assignments above still need to be covered.
