ENGL 1181 — Track A: Genres in Your Field

DRAFT

About This Track

This track focuses on genre analysis in a professional field.

Students choose a genre from a professional field they are interested in, such as police reports, patient chart notes, lesson plans, or client proposals. They gather real samples of that genre, identify the conventions that recur across them, and explain how the genre works in professional practice. A multimodal project follows, in which students apply the same rhetorical and genre concepts in a new form: a poster, a website, or a genre remix document, at the instructor's choice.

There are two projects and a final reflection. Ten writing activities scaffold them across the semester, building the essay in stages: students choose a field, find the genres used in it, narrow to one, collect samples, and identify conventions.

The professional field is the framework the assignment uses; it is not a requirement of the objectives. The track is offered in two pacings. In the portfolio model, students bundle a final revision with the reflection at the end of the semester. In the no-portfolio model, the revised draft is the graded version of Project 1.

See Units for how the readings and activities group around each project.

Required Assignments & Readings (Core)

Core assignments and readings ensure that the course outcomes and objectives are met and also help provide continuity for students taking the first-year writing sequence at Macomb. These cannot be changed without departmental approval (see ENGL 1181 Assignment Change Request Form).

Required Readings

  • 3.2 What is the Rhetorical Situation?
  • 3.3 Navigating Genres
  • 3.5 Academic Writing as a Genre
  • 3.6 Understanding Essays
  • 4.6 Revising & Orders of Concern
  • 6.1 What are Multimodal Texts?
  • 6.2 Understanding Visual Rhetoric

Also, include at least one from each of the following categories:

  • Reading strategies: At least one from Chapter 2 (2.1–2.3)
  • Writing process: At least one from Chapter 4 (4.1–4.3)
  • Critical GenAI literacy: At least 1–2 readings, including at least one from Chapter 7

Required Major Assignments

  • Project 1: Genre Analysis Essay (scaffolded across the semester)
  • Project 2: Multimodal Project (instructor chooses format: poster, website, or genre remix document)
  • Peer Review (at least one structured peer review of a draft)
  • Final Reflection (end of semester)

Recommended Assignments & Readings (Flex)

Flex assignments and readings support the core assignments and help ensure all course objectives are covered. These can be altered, replaced, or supplemented without departmental approval, as long as the core projects and objectives remain supported.

Since some of these cover the outcomes/objectives in the class as the primary assignment/activity, ensure that those are still covered if any of these assignments are altered. (See "Required Minor Assignments or In-Class Activities" section above for more information.)

Writing Activities

Writing Activities (WAs) scaffold toward Project 1 and Project 2. Instructors may modify, replace, or add writing activities as needed. See the Writing Activities page for the full list with descriptions.

Optional but recommended

There is no required number of Writing Activities — use all of them, some of them, or none at all. Unlike some flex assignments, these can be dropped entirely, not just replaced.

They're recommended because they break the major projects into steps, and each one produces something students reuse, so no project starts from a blank page. If you drop them, plan on scaffolding that work another way — the objectives listed under Required Minor Assignments above still need to be covered.
