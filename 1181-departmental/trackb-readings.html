Readings

Note to instructors

This track defines readings by the job each one does, so you can swap texts without touching any assignment. Each slot below says what the reading needs to do and what currently fills it. Assignments refer to readings by role — "the reading you annotated," "the data reading" — never by title.

Set 1 — Digital Habits and the Self

Supports Project 1 and WA1–WA2.

The data reading CORE

  • Research or survey data on how people actually use digital media.
  • Students use it to compare their own habits against a broader picture, so it needs numbers they can point at.
  • Assigned to everyone, so a freely accessible source matters.
  • Currently: Gottfried & Park, "Americans' Social Media Use 2025" (Pew Research Center).

The argument reading CORE

  • A short essay taking a clear position on digital life and its effect on relationships, attention, or sense of self.
  • This is the annotation text, so it needs a findable thesis and identifiable supporting points — WA1 asks students to highlight both.
  • Offer at least two options. The one a student picks here is the one they use in Project 1 and again in Project 3, so the options should be roughly equal in difficulty and length.
  • Currently: Sherry Turkle, "The Flight from Conversation," or Elizabeth Spiers, "I Don't Have to Tweet About My Outrage. Neither Do You." Both reach students through the library databases.

From the textbook CORE

  • Reading: 2.4 Responding to Texts — signal phrases and telling your ideas apart from an author's
  • Reading: 3.5 Academic Writing as a Genre
  • Reading: 10.4 Weaving Personal Experience into Academic Writing
  • At least one from Chapter 2 (reading strategies)
  • At least one from Chapter 4 (4.1–4.3, the writing process)

Set 2 — Digital Identity and Rhetoric

Supports Project 2 and WA4–WA5.

Digital identity readings — one or two CORE

  • How identity gets built and performed in networked spaces — why online selves are constructed, and what that costs.
  • Students draw on these to explain why the content they analyze works on its audience.
  • Currently: Jacob Babb, "Writing in the Moment: Social Media, Digital Identity, and Networked Publics" (Harlot), and danah boyd, "Excerpt: It's Complicated" (NPR).

Platform culture readings — student choice FLEX

  • First-person accounts of living inside a specific platform aesthetic or trend.
  • These give students a model for the personal-plus-analytical voice Project 2 asks for.
  • Currently: Shamani Joshi, "I Tried to be TikTok's 'That Girl' for a Week" (Vice), or Aayushi, "Instagram vs. Reality" (Medium).

From the textbook CORE

  • Reading: 3.1 What is Rhetoric?
  • Reading: 3.2 What is the Rhetorical Situation?
  • Reading: 6.1 What are Multimodal Texts?
  • Reading: 6.2 Understanding Visual Rhetoric
  • Reading: 6.3 Visual Rhetoric and Digital Writing

Set 3 — Topic Strands

Supports Project 3 and WA6.

Offer two or more strands and let students pick the topic they want to argue about. Each strand needs two readings:

  • A required reading — the one students annotate closely in WA6 and must cite in Project 3. Same requirement as Set 1: clear thesis, identifiable support.
  • A reference reading — longer, skimmed rather than read straight through, used to pull specific facts or figures into the essay.

Strands should be different enough that students are genuinely choosing a topic, not a difficulty level.

Currently two strands

  • Loneliness and connection — Evie Sun, "The Impact of Technology on Our Loneliness Epidemic" (Columbia, Center for Brain, Mind and Society) as required; the U.S. Surgeon General's advisory "Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation" as reference.
  • AI and reality — this strand can be built entirely from the textbook. Chapter 7 ends with four short news articles from The Conversation:
    • Reading: 7.5 News Article: Data Center Fights
    • Reading: 7.6 News Article: People Are Getting Their News From AI
    • Reading: 7.7 News Article: Who Owns an AI Generated Song?
    • Reading: 7.8 News Article: Taylor Swift Trademarking Her Voice
    7.6 works as the required annotation reading — it takes a position students can find. The other three serve as reference.

From the textbook CORE

  • Reading: 3.6 Understanding Essays
  • Reading: 4.6 Revising & Orders of Concern
  • A GenAI reading from Chapter 7 — 7.1 Understanding Generative AI, plus one more depending on emphasis

Set 4 — Reflection

Supports the Final Reflection and WA9.

The reflection reading CORE

  • What reflection is, why it matters to learning, and how to do it — not a personal essay, but something that gives students a vocabulary for looking back at their own work.
  • Assign it before the Final Reflection, with enough runway that students can reread their earlier work.
  • Currently: Kathleen Blake Yancey, "On Reflection," Chapter 1 of Reflection in the Writing Classroom.
