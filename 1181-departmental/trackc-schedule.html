Example 16-Week Schedule — Track C (Rhetoric & AI Ethics)

Example schedule shown for a T/Th class. This is a suggested pacing guide — instructors should adapt it to fit their section.

Three projects, each graded when due, each closing with its own reflection. Adapted from an 8-week online section; the extra weeks go into reading, drafting, and revision, not new content.

Numbered readings are chapters in the departmental OER textbook. Readings named by role — “the analysis article,” “your group’s article” — are chosen by the instructor; see Readings for what each slot needs.

Week 1 — FLEX

  • Introduce the course, expectations, syllabus, and the semester’s question about AI
  • In-class/Discussion: Syllabus Annotation — FLEX
  • Since students may still be adding/dropping this week, this week is designed to be flexible.

Week 2: What Generative AI Is

  • Reading: the GenAI foundations reading (7.1 Understanding Generative AI) — CORE
  • Reading: 2.2 How to Read Effectively — CORE (satisfies the Chapter 2 requirement)
  • In-class/Discussion: Introductions and Your Experience with GenAI — FLEX
  • WA1: Reading Annotation — GenAI Foundations — FLEX

Week 3: Rhetoric and the Rhetorical Situation

  • Reading: 3.1 What is Rhetoric? — CORE
  • Reading: 3.2 What is the Rhetorical Situation? — CORE
  • WA2: Reflecting on an AI-Generated Image — FLEX

WA2 is the first time students apply visual rhetoric, to a case where an AI system’s output carries an argument nobody wrote.

Week 4: Reading Rhetorically & Choosing a Strand

  • Reading: 2.3 How to Read Rhetorically — CORE
  • Introduce Project 1
  • Choosing Your Topic Strand — groups form here — FLEX

The strand a student picks this week follows them through all three projects.

Week 5: Reading Your Group’s Article

  • Reading: the topic-strand article for the student’s group — CORE
  • WA3: Rhetorical Reading of Your Topic Article — FLEX
  • WA4: Summary, Thoughts, and Rhetorical Reflection — FLEX

Week 6: Visual Rhetoric & Multimodality

  • Reading: 6.1 What are Multimodal Texts? — CORE
  • Reading: 6.2 Understanding Visual Rhetoric — CORE
  • Reading: 5.1 Knowing Your Audience — FLEX
  • Group Planning Space opens — FLEX

Week 7: Project 1 Due

  • Project 1 presentation posted — CORE
  • Watch and respond to every group — CORE
  • WA8: Reflecting on Issues and Group Work — FLEX

Week 8: Rhetorical Analysis & the Appeals

  • Reading: the analysis article — CORE
  • Reading: 3.6 Understanding Essays — FLEX
  • Introduce Project 2
  • Discuss ethos, pathos, and logos as analytical tools

Week 9: Building an Evaluative Claim

  • Reading: 2.4 Responding to Texts — CORE
  • WA5: Your Evaluative Claim — FLEX
  • WA6: Expanding Your Evaluative Claim — FLEX
  • Discuss signal phrases and telling your ideas apart from the author’s

Week 10: Drafting

  • Reading: one of 4.1–4.3, the writing process — CORE
  • Look at the sample essay
  • Begin drafting

Week 11: Peer Review

  • Reading: 4.4 In-Class Peer Review — FLEX
  • Project 2 first draft posted — CORE
  • Peer Review, using the class protocol and form — CORE

Week 12: Writing With GenAI

  • Reading: 7.2 Understanding AI Policies and 7.3 What’s the Point? — CORE (satisfies the Chapter 7 requirement)
  • WA9: Using a GenAI Tool for Feedback — FLEX
  • WA10: Citing Your GenAI Use — FLEX

This is where the course turns from writing about AI to writing with it. The chat transcript from WA9 is what WA10 asks students to cite.

Week 13: Revision & Project 2 Due

  • Reading: 4.5 Revising & Orders of Concern — CORE
  • Reading: 8.6 Editing and Proofreading; 12.2 MLA Formatting — FLEX
  • WA7: Revised First Draft — FLEX
  • Project 2 final draft — CORE

Week 14: Visual Persuasion

  • Reading: 6.3 Visual Rhetoric and Digital Writing — CORE
  • Revisit 6.1 and 6.2 — CORE
  • PSA support materials: what a PSA is, examples, design-tool how-to — FLEX
  • Introduce Project 3

Week 15: Prompt Workshop & Design

  • Prompt-drafting workshop — write, test, and peer-review a GenAI brainstorming prompt — FLEX
  • Design work; drop-in help

Week 16: Final Week

  • Project 3 PSA posted — CORE
  • WA11: Reflecting on Your PSA — CORE
