Project 2: Rhetorical Analysis Essay

Note to instructors

One article for the whole class works best here — it makes the peer review and the claim discussion comparable across students, and it’s how the original runs. The evaluative claim is the spine: students draft it as a single sentence in a discussion, refine it in a short write, then argue it across the essay.

Overview

You’ll write a 1,000–1,500 word essay analyzing how the author of an assigned article tries to persuade readers — and then make your own judgment about whether it works.

Analysis means describing how something works by breaking it down and examining its parts. Here the something is a piece of non-fiction writing designed to persuade. Rhetorical analysis is an advanced reading strategy for exploring how a message works in a specific situation.

Prompt

Write a paper that identifies, describes, and analyzes the rhetorical strategies the author uses.

What Your Essay Needs to Do

  • Introduction. Introduce the topic and the purpose of your paper — the ways this author tries to communicate with readers about AI and education — and foreshadow your evaluative claim. (e.g., “I argue that the author is not persuasive because she ignores her audience’s needs.”)
  • Summary and rhetorical situation. In a short paragraph, summarize the article and describe its rhetorical situation and purpose.
  • Analysis. Identify and describe three components of the author’s argument, using examples from the text for each:
    • Ethos — how the author makes themselves appear credible and trustworthy to this audience
    • Pathos — how the author appeals to the emotions and beliefs of this audience
    • Logos — how the author appeals to logic and meets the audience’s expectations for evidence
  • Claim and conclusion. Develop your own evaluative claim about the persuasive strategies in the article. Strong essays tie the claim to specific elements of the rhetorical situation — audience, purpose, context.

Outline

  • Introduction — topic and context; purpose of your essay; foreshadow the evaluative claim
  • Summary / rhetorical situation — summarize the article; describe the author’s situation and purpose
  • Analysis — ethos; pathos; logos
  • Claim / conclusion — your evaluative claim

Title

Your essay needs a title that identifies the topic and a hook that gives readers a sense of the main idea. Two examples from previous papers:

  • “Out of the Frying Pan and into the Fire: A Rhetorical Analysis of ‘Climate Change Crisis’”
  • “Playing the Logic Game: An Analysis of Logos in Edward Wilson’s ‘Apocalypse Now’”

Format

  • 1,000–1,500 words
  • MLA style; typed, double-spaced, 12 pt
  • Written in formal, edited English

Criteria for Success

  • Persuasively describes the uses of ethos, pathos, and logos in the article
  • Develops your own evaluative claim about the article
  • Meets the length and format requirements

Drafting Sequence

  1. Evaluative claim — one sentence, posted for peer response (WA5)
  2. Expand the claim with evidence from the article (WA6)
  3. Complete first draft, posted for peer review
  4. Peer review using the class protocol and form
  5. Revised draft (WA7)
  6. GenAI feedback on organization, plus a reflection on what it was and wasn’t useful for (WA9)
  7. Cite your GenAI use (WA10)
  8. Final draft, revised using instructor feedback and whichever GenAI feedback you chose to act on

Nothing may be copied and pasted from a GenAI tool. You consider the feedback and decide whether to apply it.
