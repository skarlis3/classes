Project 1: Group Presentation on an Issue in AI Ethics

Note to instructors

This is a collaborative, narrated presentation posted where the whole class can watch and respond. Groups form around the topic strand each student chooses, so the grouping mechanism and the reading choice are the same decision. Each student owns one of three sections, which keeps the division of labor explicit and gradable.

Overview

With your group, you’ll teach the class about the issue in AI ethics your group chose. You’ll build a narrated presentation, post it where everyone can see it, and then watch and respond to every other group’s work.

Your job is to teach us about your topic.

Your Group’s Three Sections

The following is distributed between group members — each person takes one:

  • Ethical issue. Describe the issue your group explored, introduce the title of the shared article, the author’s main argument, and their reasons for why this is an issue. Summarize the key information.
  • Examples. Share the most relevant example, story, or anecdote the author uses, and describe which of the author’s reasons is the most convincing and why.
  • Rhetorical reading. Discuss what you gathered from your rhetorical reading. Explain why your group agrees or disagrees with the author’s viewpoint based on what you learned.

Presentation Requirements

  • Each presenter is in charge of one section and must have at least one slide. There’s no limit on slides, but keep them concise.
  • Record your screen and voice using the tool your instructor specifies. We must be able to see your slides. Including your face is welcome but not required.
  • Your slides should demonstrate what you learned about visual rhetoric and multimodality.
  • Each presenter should speak for about 2 minutes — try not to go over three.
  • Cover the material you’re responsible for, and support your groupmates with feedback where you can.

Engaging With Your Peers

  • Watch every group’s presentation.
  • Respond thoughtfully and respectfully. Your response should show you carefully considered their content, highlight something you found interesting or thought-provoking, and ask a question or offer constructive feedback that keeps the conversation going.

Peer engagement is part of your grade. Thoughtful, substantive responses earn full points; minimal or superficial participation earns fewer.

Criteria for Success

  • Content — clearly explain your assigned section with accurate and complete information
  • Organization & clarity — present ideas logically and make them easy to follow
  • Slide quality & design — a clear, readable slide that supports what you’re saying
  • Delivery & engagement — speak confidently, at a good pace
  • Collaboration — contribute actively and coordinate so the presentation holds together
  • Peer engagement — respond in a way that shows you engaged with each group’s content

What Feeds This

  • The rhetorical reading you did of your group’s article (WA3)
  • Your summary and rhetorical reflection (WA4)
  • The visual rhetoric and multimodality reading, which your slide design is graded against
