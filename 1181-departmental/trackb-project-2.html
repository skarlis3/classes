Project 2: The Digital Rhetoric Essay

Note to instructors

This is the most transferable assignment in the track — students supply their own text, so it never goes stale. They choose it from what they logged in WA5. The analysis moves from "what is this" to "how does it work" to "what does it do to me."

Overview

You'll write a rhetorical analysis of one specific piece of digital media from your own feed. Project 1 looked at your digital habits in general; here you narrow to a single text and take it apart to see how it works on the people who see it.

Choosing Your Text

Pick one piece of content from your own scrolling — a short video, a post, a thread, a trend, or a specific creator's whole look and feel.

Choose something that genuinely made you stop scrolling. Straightforward advertisements are usually a weak choice; they're too easy to read.

Most students choose from what they recorded in the Scrolling Log.

Choosing Your Audience

Same three options as Project 1 — peers, family or elders, the college community. Choosing differently this time is a good idea; it will make you notice how much the audience changes what you write.

Prompt

Analyze how your chosen content uses multimodal elements — sound, image, editing, and text — to persuade the people who see it. There's no single way to write a rhetorical analysis, but yours should do the following.

What Your Essay Needs to Do

  • Set the context. Describe your relationship to the platform. What kind of content usually reaches you there, and why do you think this piece did? What did you feel the first time you saw it?
  • Lay out the rhetorical situation. Summarize what's happening in the content, then work through:
    • The creator — what's their persona, and how do they build credibility?
    • The audience — who is this clearly aimed at, and how can you tell?
    • The timing — why is this relevant now? What trend or moment is it part of?
  • Analyze how it works. Go past what the content says to how it's built, and whether it succeeds.
    • Sound — how does audio shape the mood, and does that serve the message?
    • Image — how do lighting, color, framing, and editing hold your attention and build meaning?
    • Persuasion — how does it hook you in the first few seconds, and what does it want from you at the end? Did it work on you?
  • Connect to a reading. How does your analysis connect to one of the digital identity readings? Use one or two quotes from it.
  • Close by turning it back on yourself. How does consuming this kind of content shape your thinking and behavior? Does digital rhetoric persuade you, empower you, or manipulate you? How does that compare to what you said in Project 1?

Using the Readings

Use one of the digital identity readings.

Use only assigned course readings. No outside sources.

Writing in Your Own Voice

  • Use "I."
  • Don't perform "academic."
  • Be honest. Say what the content actually does to you, and whether you think it's effective — not what you think an analysis is supposed to say.

Format

  • MLA style
  • Minimum 1,000 words
  • In-text citations
  • Works Cited page, including a link to the content you analyzed

Drafting Sequence

  1. Rough draft (minimum 700 words)
  2. Peer reading — read two classmates' drafts and respond using YEPs / NOPEs / WOWs
  3. Final draft
  4. Optional revision, with a written account of what you changed and why
