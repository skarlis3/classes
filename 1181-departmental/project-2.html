Project 2: Multimodal Project

Overview

You'll create a multimodal text that demonstrates your understanding of visual rhetoric and genre conventions. This project connects to your work from Project 1 — you'll apply the rhetorical and genre concepts you've been working with all semester to create something in a new genre.

Assignment & Format Options

Note to instructorsChoose one of the three format options below for your section, then delete the other two before distributing to students.

Option 1: Poster Presentation

Create a physical multimodal display and present it to the class. Your poster must demonstrate intentional design choices — layout, typography, color, images, and organization should all be purposeful, not random.

Option 2: Website

Build a small website (using Google Sites or a similar tool). Your site must demonstrate intentional design choices — think about how layout, color, typography, and navigation shape how your audience experiences the content.

Option 3: Genre Remix Document

Create a multimodal document that remixes your genre analysis work into a new genre. For example: a guide to writing in your field, a "how to build a professional website for your [field] business" resource, a brochure, or an infographic. This needs to be more than a plain Word document — your design choices should be intentional and visible.

Option 4: Genre Remix Document (Alternate)

Create a multimodal document that remixes one of the genre examples from your field into a new genre. For example: a business report that proposed a change to a department from your field could become a company blog article about the exciting new things happening at Company X. This needs to be more than a plain Word document — your design choices should be intentional and visible.

Requirements

No matter which format you're working in, your project must:

  • Demonstrate your understanding of visual rhetoric through intentional layout, design, and typography choices
  • Combine text with visual or design elements (not just a text-only document)
  • Connect to concepts from the course — genre, rhetoric, audience, purpose
  • Show evidence of planning and intentional choices
